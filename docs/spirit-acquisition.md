# Spirit acquisition

Better Spirit Commerce uses the pinned, unmodified Malum mob spirit mappings (including Malum's configured category defaults). An eligible player-credited kill releases Malum's actual spirit types and counts immediately at the dying mob. Its seven ordinary native aspects map to five **physical shards that are both trade currencies and Malum reagents**: Infernal + Arcane → Power; Aerial → Motion; Aqueous + Earthen → Endurance; Sacred → Renewal; Wicked → Control. Converging untagged drops sum their quantities once and split at item stack limits; tagged shard NBT retains its identity rather than being combined with an untagged shard. Eldritch and Umbral remain separate specialist native shards. Thus the physical economy has five core plus two specialists, while Malum's original internal type identifiers remain available for blockstates, visuals, indexes, and ritual selection. An empty Malum result yields no synthetic spirit drop.

The existing exclusions still apply to player victims, soulless or spawner-origin mobs, villagers, iron golems, and the tagged economy actors. Malum's equipment-gated death handler remains suppressed to avoid duplicate drops; Malum's mappings, not equipment eligibility, choose the types. Dropped spirits use Malum's owner-targeted floating items and lose their temporary grouping tag on collection.

Tempo is not a sixth registered core shard or market. Malum defines no Tempo mob mapping; there is **no new Tempo supply, synthetic Motion drop, or dual payout**. Aerial's original quantity contributes to Motion once. Existing recipe IDs and nonspirit costs are retained except the previously retired equipment and obsolete self-to-self refinement adapters. Nine native recipe pairs have first-match output collisions after reagent consolidation: the [canonical five-spirit recipe follow-up release hold](../../../better-content-modpack/docs/design.md#five-spirit-recipe-follow-up-release-hold) lists the exact affected IDs. This source work is not a release qualification.

This implementation has no delayed delivery, saved credit balance, delivery receipt, or upgrade payout. It targets a **fresh shared world**; no old-world migration, remap or deletion was attempted, and older queued credits must not be assumed recoverable. Retaining native internal identifiers is a boundary-safety choice, not proof of backwards-compatible inventories or registries. Restore a backup with the earlier mod set if an existing world requires its old behavior. All authored JUnit/GameTests and native client/world checks are **UNRUN**; compilation alone does not establish drop, ritual, recipe-selection or save behavior.
